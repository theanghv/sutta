## The four brahmavihāras

*Brahmavihārā · Four divine abodes*

**Purpose:** Develop an expansive heart through love, compassion, rejoicing, and equanimity. These qualities inform both meditation and how we meet other beings.

| Quality | Pāli | Direction of practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Love / goodwill | Mettā | Wish for beings’ welfare and happiness; extend goodwill beyond personal preference. |
| Compassion | Karuṇā | Meet suffering with concern and the wish for its relief. |
| Rejoicing | Muditā | Welcome others’ well-being and good fortune. |
| [[#Equanimity — Upekkhā\|Equanimity]] | Upekkhā | Remain balanced and open-hearted as circumstances change. |

**Practice reminder:** Let the concern for well-being widen beyond the people it is easiest to care for.

**Read with a purpose**

- [[Sutta texts/MN 62#^brahmaviharas|MN 62 — The Longer Advice to Rāhula]] — What each quality helps us abandon: ill will, cruelty, discontent, and aversion.
- [[Sutta texts/DN 13#^brahmaviharas|DN 13 — Experts in the Three Vedas]] — How to extend each quality in every direction, abundantly and without limit.
- [[Sutta texts/Snp 1.8#Text|Snp 1.8 — The Discourse on Love]] — Boundless goodwill supported by conduct and carried through daily life.

Continue through [[Practices/Mettā and the brahmavihāras|Mettā and the brahmavihāras]].

## The five faculties

*Pañcindriyāni · Capacities to develop*

**Purpose:** Strengthen the capacities that sustain and deepen the path. Here “faculties” means faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom.

| Faculty | Pāli | Direction of practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Faith | Saddhā | Develop confidence in the Buddha’s awakening and the possibility of the path. |
| Energy | Viriya | Give up unskillful qualities; bring forth and sustain skillful qualities. |
| Mindfulness | Sati | Remember the teaching and maintain awareness of body, feelings, mind, and principles. |
| Immersion / samādhi | Samādhi | Let the mind gather and unify through letting go. |
| Wisdom | Paññā | Understand arising and passing away, suffering, and its ending. |

**Practice reminder:** Notice which capacity needs support in the present situation, and return to the instructions that develop it.

**Read with a purpose**

- [SN 48.10 — Analysis (2nd)](https://suttacentral.net/sn48.10/en/sujato) — Defines each faculty and connects it with concrete training. *Online reference; not yet in the collection.*
- [[Sutta texts/MN 118#Text|MN 118 — Mindfulness of Breathing]] — A sustained practice in which mindfulness develops through investigation, energy, and the other awakening factors.

**Energy in practice:** [[Practices/Energy (Viriya)|Energy — Viriya]] connects this faculty with working through dullness and learning balanced effort. [[Sutta texts/AN 6.55#^balanced-energy|AN 6.55 — With Soṇa]] illustrates the balance of energy and serenity.

## The five hindrances

*Pañca nīvaraṇāni · Obstructions to recognize and release*

**Purpose:** Recognize the conditions obstructing the mind, understand how they arise, and learn how they are abandoned.

| Hindrance | Pāli | What to recognize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sensual desire | Kāmacchanda | The mind drawn into wanting sensual gratification. |
| Ill will | Byāpāda | Hostility, resentment, or wishing harm. |
| Dullness and drowsiness | Thinamiddha | Mental dullness and sinking into sleepiness. |
| Restlessness and remorse | Uddhaccakukkucca | Agitation and being troubled by what has been done or left undone. |
| Doubt | Vicikicchā | Uncertainty that obstructs confidence in proceeding with the training. |

**Practice reminder from MN 10:** Know when a hindrance is present or absent; understand its arising, its abandonment, and how its future arising is prevented.

**Read with a purpose**

- [[Sutta texts/MN 10#4.1. The Hindrances|MN 10 — Mindfulness Meditation]] — The full framework for recognizing and understanding the five hindrances.
- [[Sutta texts/MN 19#Text|MN 19 — Two Kinds of Thought]] — Recognize thoughts by their consequences and understand how repeated thinking inclines the mind.
- [[Sutta texts/MN 20#Text|MN 20 — How to Stop Thinking]] — Five approaches for dealing with persistent unskillful thoughts.

Continue through [[Practices/Abandoning unskillful thoughts|Abandoning unskillful thoughts]] and [[Practices/Energy (Viriya)|Energy — Viriya]]. [[Sutta texts/SN 46.51#Text|SN 46.51 — Nourishing]] explains what feeds or weakens all five hindrances and the seven awakening factors.

## The five aggregates

*Pañca khandhā · Aspects of experience to understand*

**Purpose:** Examine experience, its conditions, and the ways we grasp it as “mine,” “I,” or “my self.”

| Aggregate | Pāli | Starting point for understanding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Rūpa | Material form: the four principal elements and form derived from them. |
| Feeling | Vedanā | The pleasant, painful, or neutral tone of experience. |
| Perception | Saññā | Recognizing and distinguishing what is experienced. |
| Choices | Saṅkhārā | Intentional activity; SN 22.56 explains this through six classes of intention. |
| Consciousness | Viññāṇa | Knowing through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. |

**Practice reminder:** Attend to an aggregate’s arising and disappearance, and notice how identification with it gives rise to distress.

**Read with a purpose**

- [[Sutta texts/SN 22.56#Text|SN 22.56 — Rounds of the Grasping Aggregates]] — Definitions, conditions, cessation, and the path.
- [[Sutta texts/SN 22.1#Text|SN 22.1 — Nakula’s Father]] — How identification with changing aggregates afflicts the mind, and how this affliction is avoided.
- [[Sutta texts/SN 22.59#Text|SN 22.59 — The Characteristic of Not-Self]] — Examine each aggregate through affliction, impermanence, and its suitability for identification as self.

Continue through [[Practices/Contemplation of the aggregates|Contemplation of the aggregates]], [[Practices/Feelings and impermanence|Feelings and impermanence]], and [[Practices/Consciousness and the six senses|Consciousness and the six senses]].

## The elements

*Dhātu · The five elements in MN 62*

**Purpose:** Understand the body in terms of its material qualities and spaces, recognizing the same elements internally and externally and relinquishing ownership.

| Element | Pāli | Examples in MN 62 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earth | Pathavī | Hard and solid aspects: hair, teeth, bones, and flesh. |
| Water | Āpo | Watery aspects: blood, sweat, tears, and other fluids. |
| Fire | Tejo | Warmth, bodily heat, and digestion. |
| Air | Vāyo | Bodily winds and in-breaths and out-breaths. |
| Space | Ākāsa | Openings and cavities: ears, nostrils, mouth, and digestive spaces. |

**Practice reminder:** Recognize the element internally and externally as the same element, and contemplate relinquishing “mine,” “I,” and “my self.”

**Read with a purpose**

- [[Sutta texts/MN 62#^elements|MN 62 — The Longer Advice to Rāhula]] — The five-element contemplation, followed by using the elements as models for a mind not overwhelmed by pleasant and unpleasant contact.
- [[Sutta texts/MN 140#^element-analysis|MN 140 — The Analysis of the Elements]] — Extends the analysis to six elements, including consciousness, and continues into feeling, equanimity, and letting go.

Continue through [[Practices/Element contemplation|Element contemplation]].

## Dependent origination

*Paṭiccasamuppāda · Understanding conditions and cessation*

**Purpose:** Understand the conditions through which suffering develops and ceases, and how supporting conditions allow the path to unfold.

The twelve links are **ignorance → choices → consciousness → name and form → six sense fields → contact → feeling → craving → grasping → continued existence → rebirth → old age and death**. [[Practices/Dependent origination|Dependent origination]] gives English and Pāli terms, condensed definitions, and the cessation sequence.

**Practice reminder:** Recognize contact and feeling, observe the movement toward craving and grasping, and understand what supports relinquishing these tendencies.

**Read with a purpose**

- [[Sutta texts/SN 12.2#Text|SN 12.2 — Analysis]] — The definitions of the twelve links and their arising and cessation.
- [[Sutta texts/SN 12.15#Text|SN 12.15 — Kaccānagotta]] — Right view through conditional arising and cessation, relinquishing fixation on “my self”.
- [[Sutta texts/SN 12.23#Text|SN 12.23 — Vital Conditions]] — The conditions for faith, joy, tranquility, immersion, and freedom.

Continue through [[Practices/Dependent origination|Dependent origination]], with [[Sutta texts/MN 38#Text|MN 38 — The Longer Discourse on the Ending of Craving]] and [[Sutta texts/MN 148#Text|MN 148 — Six By Six]] already supplying further connections to consciousness, feeling, and craving.

## Equanimity — Upekkhā

*Worked example of an individual teaching note*

**At its heart:** A balanced, attentive mind that can remain open to what is happening. In relationships, cultivate this balance together with goodwill and compassion. In meditation, learn to recognize when the mind is settled and allow that balance to deepen.

### A reflection to return to

> I have immense love and goodwill for you, but I also recognize that I cannot control your path or your choices.

*A reflection from our discussion, rather than a quotation from a sutta.*

The practical movement is to keep caring while releasing the demand to determine another person’s outcome. Offer help where it is useful. When further intervention would only express your own agitation, remain present and give the situation room.

### Close watching

“Close watching” is a reminder for engaged, balanced attention. In MN 118, Sujato translates the development of equanimity as:

> They closely watch over that mind immersed in samādhi.

— [[Sutta texts/MN 118#Text|MN 118 — Mindfulness of Breathing]]

The passage concerns a mind established in samādhi. The reflection about relationships above applies this attentive steadiness in the context of goodwill and compassion. It is a practical interpretation, not a literal translation of the Pāli word.

### Two contexts to explore

| Context | How to understand the practice | Passage to return to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The brahmavihāras | Extend a heart of equanimity toward beings, abundantly and without limit. | [[Sutta texts/DN 13#^brahmaviharas\|DN 13 — Experts in the Three Vedas]] |
| The awakening factors | Closely watch over a mind immersed in samādhi, developing equanimity as an awakening factor. | [[Sutta texts/MN 118#Text\|MN 118 — Mindfulness of Breathing]] |

### Bringing it into practice

- **With another person:** Recognize their suffering or difficulty, keep goodwill present, and distinguish useful help from the urge to control.
- **When resentment appears:** Develop equanimity toward that person. This is one of the methods in [[Sutta texts/AN 5.161#Text|AN 5.161 — Getting Rid of Resentment (1st)]].
- **When meditation is settled:** Stay attentive to the settled mind. Notice whether additional adjustment is actually needed.

Equanimity can be cultivated along the way and deepen as the heart and mind develop. It is the fourth brahmavihāra and the seventh awakening factor; these positions do not mean that practice must wait until everything preceding it is perfected.

**Recognizing the right occasion:** [[Sutta texts/SN 46.53#Text|SN 46.53 — Fire]] recommends equanimity, tranquility, and immersion for a restless mind, and investigation, energy, and rapture for a sluggish one. Continue through [[Practices/Energy (Viriya)|Energy — Viriya]] to explore this balance.

### A further image

In [[Sutta texts/MN 140#Text|MN 140 — The Analysis of the Elements]], a goldsmith sometimes fans the gold, sometimes sprinkles it with water, and sometimes simply watches it. The image leads into equanimity described as pure, bright, pliable, workable, and radiant.

**Working reminder:** Care, attend, and respond to what is needed. Learn to recognize when watchful steadiness itself is the appropriate response.
