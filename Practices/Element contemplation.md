Contemplating the body in terms of its elements, recognizing internal and external elements, and examining the claim that they are mine, me, or my self.

## What each discourse contributes

- [[Sutta texts/MN 28#^form-and-elements|MN 28 — The Longer Simile of the Elephant’s Footprint]] — Situates the four elements within the form aggregate, compares their internal and external forms, and applies the contemplation to impermanence and the response to painful contact.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 62#^elements|MN 62 — The Longer Advice to Rāhula]] — Describes earth, water, fire, air, and space internally and externally. It also uses the elements as models for a mind that is not overwhelmed by agreeable and disagreeable contact.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 140#^element-analysis|MN 140 — The Analysis of the Elements]] — Develops the analysis through six elements, including consciousness, and continues into feeling, equanimity, and relinquishing grasping.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 10#1.5. Focusing on the Elements|MN 10 — Mindfulness Meditation]] — Gives the concise instruction to examine the body according to the four elements, within contemplation of the body.


- [[Sutta texts/SN 18.9#Text|SN 18.9 — Elements]] — Applies the brief impermanence-and-not-self examination to all six elements, including space and consciousness.
