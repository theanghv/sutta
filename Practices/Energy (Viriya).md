# Energy — Viriya

Cultivate a bright, engaged mind and learn to balance energy with settling. These texts connect recognizing dullness and drowsiness, developing energy, and choosing the response appropriate to the present mind.

## What each discourse contributes

- [[Sutta texts/SN 46.51#^nourishing-energy|SN 46.51 — Nourishing]] — Explains what feeds or weakens the hindrances and awakening factors. Initiative, persistence, and exertion both nourish energy and weaken dullness and drowsiness.

- [[Sutta texts/SN 46.53#^energizing-a-sluggish-mind|SN 46.53 — Fire]] — Develop investigation, energy, and rapture when the mind is sluggish; tranquility, immersion, and equanimity when it is restless. Mindfulness is useful throughout.

- [[Sutta texts/AN 7.61#^working-with-drowsiness|AN 7.61 — Nodding Off]] — Successive practical responses to nodding off while meditating, including changes in attention, physical activity, walking, and mindful rest.

- [[Sutta texts/AN 6.55#^balanced-energy|AN 6.55 — With Soṇa]] — The harp-string analogy for energy that is neither too forceful nor too slack, balanced with serenity and the faculties.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 10#4.1. The Hindrances|MN 10 — Mindfulness Meditation]] — Recognize dullness and drowsiness, their arising and abandonment, and the development of energy as an awakening factor.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 118#Text|MN 118 — Mindfulness of Breathing]] — Shows investigation rousing energy, followed by rapture, tranquility, immersion, and equanimity within the development of awakening factors.

## Initiative, persistence, and exertion

[[Sutta texts/SN 46.51#^nourishing-energy|SN 46.51 — Nourishing]] identifies these three elements as nourishment for energy. Rational application of mind to them also weakens dullness and drowsiness.

**Practice reminder:** Recognize opportunities to begin skillful cultivation, sustain it, and apply the effort it needs.

## Recognize what the mind needs

This distinction follows [[Sutta texts/SN 46.53#Text|SN 46.53 — Fire]].

| Present condition | Awakening factors to develop |
| --- | --- |
| Sluggish | Investigation of principles, energy, and rapture. |
| Restless | Tranquility, immersion, and equanimity. |

Mindfulness is useful in both situations. Observe the effect of the cultivation as the condition of the mind changes.

## When you are nodding off

[[Sutta texts/AN 7.61#Text|AN 7.61 — Nodding Off]] gives successive responses, moving on when the preceding approach does not work:

1. Stop focusing on and cultivating the perception present when drowsiness arose.
2. Think about and examine the teaching you have learned.
3. Rehearse that teaching in detail.
4. Pinch the ears and rub the limbs.
5. Get up, wash the eyes with water, look around, and look at the stars and constellations.
6. Apply the mind to the perception of light and develop a radiant mind.
7. Walk mindfully, keeping the mind from scattering outside.
8. If needed, lie down on the right side, mindful and aware, with attention to the time of getting up.

The list is a condensed reading guide to the source passage.

## Balanced energy

In [[Sutta texts/AN 6.55#^balanced-energy|AN 6.55 — With Soṇa]], strings that are too tight or too slack cannot be played well. Energy that is too forceful leads to restlessness; energy that is too slack leads to laziness. The instruction is to attend to energy and serenity, balance the faculties, and learn the character of the situation.

**Practice reminder:** Recognize when effort needs strengthening, when it needs softening, and whether the mind is becoming clearer and steadier.
