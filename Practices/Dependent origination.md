# Dependent origination — Paṭiccasamuppāda

Understand the conditions sustaining suffering and how their cessation leads to freedom. This connects the study of consciousness, the senses, feeling, craving, and grasping with the development of the path.

## What each discourse contributes

- [[Sutta texts/SN 12.2#^twelve-links|SN 12.2 — Analysis]] — The main reference for the twelve links, their definitions, and the arising and cessation of suffering.

- [[Sutta texts/SN 12.15#^right-view-and-self|SN 12.15 — Kaccānagotta]] — Connects dependent origination with right view and relinquishing fixation on “my self”.

- [[Sutta texts/SN 12.23#^conditions-for-suffering-and-freedom|SN 12.23 — Vital Conditions]] — Connects the conditions for suffering with the conditions for faith, joy, tranquility, immersion, and freedom.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 38#Text|MN 38 — The Longer Discourse on the Ending of Craving]] — Already contains the twelve-link arising and cessation sequences and explains the conditional arising of consciousness.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 148#Text|MN 148 — Six By Six]] — Examines the senses, consciousness, contact, feeling, and craving, and how the responses to feeling sustain or abandon underlying tendencies.

## The twelve links

The sequence and definitions below follow [[Sutta texts/SN 12.2#Text|SN 12.2 — Analysis]]. The explanations in the table are condensed reading notes.

| Link | Pāli | Meaning in SN 12.2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ignorance | Avijjā | Not knowing suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path to cessation. |
| Choices | Saṅkhārā | Choices by way of body, speech, and mind. |
| Consciousness | Viññāṇa | The six classes of consciousness. |
| Name and form | Nāmarūpa | Name: feeling, perception, intention, contact, and application of mind. Form: the four principal elements and derived form. |
| Six sense fields | Saḷāyatana | Eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. |
| Contact | Phassa | Contact through each of the six senses. |
| Feeling | Vedanā | Feeling born of contact through each of the six senses. |
| Craving | Taṇhā | Craving for sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas. |
| Grasping | Upādāna | Grasping at sensual pleasures, views, precepts and observances, and theories of self. |
| Continued existence | Bhava | Existence in the sensual realm, the realm of luminous form, and the formless realm. |
| Rebirth | Jāti | Rebirth, manifestation of the aggregates, and acquisition of the sense fields. |
| Old age and death | Jarāmaraṇa | Aging and death, with the sorrow, pain, and distress named in the sequence. |

Each link is presented as a requirement for the next. The cessation sequence begins with ignorance fading away and ceasing, and proceeds through the cessation of the remaining links to the cessation of suffering.

## A place to investigate in practice

**Contact → feeling → craving → grasping.**

Recognize what is felt and observe whether the mind moves into wanting, resisting, or holding on. [[Sutta texts/MN 148#Text|MN 148 — Six By Six]] examines how the responses to pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling relate to the underlying tendencies to greed, aversion, and ignorance, and how those tendencies are abandoned.

**Practice reminder:** Attend to the conditions of the experience, the response that follows, and what supports relinquishing that response.

## Conditions supporting freedom

In [[Sutta texts/SN 12.23#Text|SN 12.23 — Vital Conditions]], the sequence continues from suffering into the conditions supporting the path:

**Suffering → faith → joy → rapture → tranquility → bliss → immersion → truly knowing and seeing → disillusionment → dispassion → freedom → knowledge of ending.**

This connects dependent origination with [[Practices/Joy and settling the mind|Joy and settling the mind]]: cultivation and understanding develop through supporting conditions.
