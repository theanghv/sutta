Recognizing and abandoning unskillful thinking and the mental qualities that sustain it. The collection connects the recognition of harmful thinking with ways to abandon it, alongside working with resentment, ill will, and the hindrances.

## What each discourse contributes

- [[Sutta texts/AN 5.161#Text|AN 5.161 — Getting Rid of Resentment (1st)]] — Offers five ways to abandon resentment toward a person: love, compassion, equanimity, disengaging attention, and remembering ownership of actions.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 62#^brahmaviharas|MN 62 — The Longer Advice to Rāhula]] — Provides specific meditations for abandoning ill will, cruelty, discontent, and aversion; the following instructions also address lust and the conceit “I am”.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 10#4.1. The Hindrances|MN 10 — Mindfulness Meditation]] — Directs attention to whether a hindrance is present, how it arises, how it is abandoned, and how its future arising is prevented.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 19#^two-kinds-of-thought|MN 19 — Two Kinds of Thought]] — Distinguishes harmful thinking from renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness; shows how frequent thinking inclines the mind, and how even wholesome thinking can be settled for immersion.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 20#^five-methods|MN 20 — How to Stop Thinking]] — Gives five successive methods to use when unskillful thoughts persist: attend to a skillful subject, examine the drawbacks, forget and ignore, stop their formation, and finally apply forceful restraint.
