Examining the knowing of experience itself: how consciousness arises dependent on conditions, changes, and is included in the contemplation of not-self. This addresses the felt sense of being the one who experiences, beyond questions about personality or one’s life story.

## What each discourse contributes

- [[Sutta texts/SN 22.1#^consciousness-and-distress|SN 22.1 — Nakula’s Father]] — Examines consciousness as self, self as possessing consciousness, consciousness within self, or self within consciousness, and relates this identification to distress when consciousness changes.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 38#^the-one-who-knows|MN 38 — The Longer Discourse on the Ending of Craving]] — Directly challenges Sāti’s claim that the same consciousness transmigrates. His description of the one who speaks, knows, and experiences is followed by the Buddha’s explanation of consciousness arising dependent on conditions.

- [[Sutta texts/SN 35.93#^dependent-consciousness|SN 35.93 — A Duality (2nd)]] — Connects the impermanence of the sense faculty and object to the impermanence of consciousness. Their meeting is contact; feeling, intention, and perception are also examined as changing.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 148#^consciousness-not-self|MN 148 — Six By Six]] — Lays out six sets of six: sense faculties, objects, consciousness, contact, feeling, and craving. Its not-self argument includes the evident arising and vanishing of consciousness itself.

- [[Sutta texts/SN 18.3#Text|SN 18.3 — Consciousness]] — The compact examination of all six kinds of consciousness within the SN 18 framework.

- [[Sutta texts/SN 22.59#Text|SN 22.59 — The Characteristic of Not-Self]] — Includes consciousness in both the affliction-and-control argument and the examination of impermanence, suffering, and not-self.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 140#^element-analysis|MN 140 — The Analysis of the Elements]] — Includes consciousness in the six-element analysis and continues through feeling, equanimity, and relinquishing grasping.

## The six kinds of consciousness

The sense faculty and object are conditions for the corresponding consciousness. They do not turn into consciousness. [[Sutta texts/MN 38#^conditioned-consciousness|MN 38 — The Longer Discourse on the Ending of Craving]] and [[Sutta texts/MN 148#^six-kinds-of-consciousness|MN 148 — Six By Six]] name the six kinds:

| Sense faculty | Object | Consciousness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eye | Sights | Eye consciousness |
| Ear | Sounds | Ear consciousness |
| Nose | Smells | Nose consciousness |
| Tongue | Tastes | Tongue consciousness |
| Body | Touches | Body consciousness |
| Mind | Ideas | Mind consciousness |

## A route through the question

Read [[Sutta texts/MN 38#Text|MN 38 — The Longer Discourse on the Ending of Craving]] for the claim about the enduring experiencer and the account of conditioned consciousness. Follow with [[Sutta texts/SN 35.93#Text|SN 35.93 — A Duality (2nd)]] to examine why the knowing, as well as the known object, is impermanent. Read [[Sutta texts/MN 148#Text|MN 148 — Six By Six]] for the explicit not-self examination across the whole process. [[Sutta texts/SN 18.3#Text|SN 18.3 — Consciousness]] then provides a concise reference for returning to the contemplation.
