Cultivating love or goodwill, compassion, rejoicing, and equanimity. The collection includes the attitude to develop, the way to extend it, and its application to resentment.

## What each discourse contributes

- [[Sutta texts/Snp 1.8#Text|Snp 1.8 — The Discourse on Love]] — The concise reference for boundless goodwill toward beings, supported by conduct and maintained through the different postures of daily life.

- [[Sutta texts/DN 13#^brahmaviharas|DN 13 — Experts in the Three Vedas]] — The main directional formula for extending each of the four brahmavihāras abundantly and without limit.

- [[Sutta texts/AN 3.65#^brahmaviharas|AN 3.65 — With the Kālāmas of Kesamutta]] — Connects the four brahmavihāras with freedom from greed, hatred, and confusion, and describes the assurances of living without enmity and ill will.

- [[Sutta texts/AN 5.161#Text|AN 5.161 — Getting Rid of Resentment (1st)]] — Applies love, compassion, and equanimity to resentment toward a particular person, alongside two further ways to let resentment go.

- [[Sutta texts/MN 62#^brahmaviharas|MN 62 — The Longer Advice to Rāhula]] — Pairs these meditations with the qualities they help abandon: ill will, cruelty, discontent, and aversion.
